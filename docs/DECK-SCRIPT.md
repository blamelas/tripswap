# TripSwap — 10-minute deck script (English)

**Deck:** [`TripSwap-Defense.pptx`](TripSwap-Defense.pptx) · **PDF:** [`TripSwap-Defense.pdf`](TripSwap-Defense.pdf)
**Format:** 10 slides, 16:9, English. **Target:** 9:30, leaving 30 seconds for the handover to Q&A.

---

## 0:00 — Slide 1 · Title

> “Good afternoon. I’m Bruno, and this is TripSwap.
> Small changes, bigger journeys.
> It is a travel discovery product that turns everyday swaps into future experiences — with the real cost always visible.
> Eight destinations, twenty-five swaps, six categories, forty-five tests. The URL is at the bottom.”

## 0:40 — Slide 2 · The problem

> “The problem is not that people do not know where they want to go.
> The gap is knowing whether they can afford it.
> Budget tools start with what you already spent; spreadsheets ask for salary and statements; inspiration shows photographs and prices.
> None of them turns desire into a plan with a timeline. That is the missing bridge.”

## 1:20 — Slide 3 · The idea

> “TripSwap starts with the destination and reveals the choices that could fund it.
> The €0* is a metaphor: a cost covered by swaps, never a discount and never a free trip.
> The real planning target stays visible: €650 for Paris.
> The three pillars are practical: spend smarter, live healthier, consume consciously. All voluntary.”

## 2:00 — Slide 4 · The experience

> “The flow is discover, swap, unlock.
> In Paris, Balanced: coffee at home, lunch at home, half the Ubers. Three swaps, €122 a month, six months.
> The reward card transforms in place: the same photograph, the same height, the coral state, the horizon buttons and the simulation note.
> No extra section; no layout jump.”

## 3:00 — Slide 5 · Scope

> “The MVP is four stories: discover, select, understand, save.
> In: eight destinations, twenty-five swaps, six categories, the deterministic engine, overlap groups, the swaps catalogue, saved plans, PT/EN and calendar reminders.
> Out: authentication, booking, payments, live fares, maps, push notifications and a backend.
> The test is simple: without this, does the user still get the core value? If yes, it stays out.”

## 3:50 — Slide 6 · Architecture and design

> “It is a static SPA: React 19, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind, deployed on Vercel.
> Typed data goes into a pure engine; the UI reads the result; localStorage stores the plans. No server, no accounts, no live prices.
> The design direction is ‘The world is the reward’: canvas, ink, one signal colour, Barlow Condensed and IBM Plex.
> Photography is the interface; the catalogue does not turn into a dashboard.”

## 4:40 — Slide 7 · Calculation and guardrails

> “Every number is reproducible.
> Recurring savings are (original − alternative) × frequency. One-time savings are counted once. The horizon is the first whole month at the target.
> Overlap groups replace, never sum. Games of chance use a fixed conservative estimate, €40 → €20 = €20/month; no inputs, no invented average.
> The receipt shows the formula. The result is not generated by AI.”

## 5:40 — Slide 8 · The swaps catalogue

> “The swaps are the actions; the destination is the reward.
> Twenty-five changes in six categories, independent of any destination.
> You select swaps, see monthly and one-time savings separately, and carry the selection into any plan through the links and the global selection.
> Games of chance use the same conservative estimate: €40 current, €20 reduced, €20 a month. The roadmap cards are deliberately not interactive.”

## 6:40 — Slide 9 · Process with AI

> “The repository is the evidence: 110+ conventional commits, 20+ merged PRs, 42 tests, three agents and three skills.
> CLAUDE.md carries the stack, the rules and the non-negotiables. ITERATIONS records the cycles; PROMPTS records the real prompts.
> Two AI failures matter. The engine summed overlapping swaps; a test failed with two lines instead of one, and I fixed the engine. A literal i18n type broke the English dictionary; tsc caught it, and I widened the type.
> CI runs on every PR; Vercel deploys main.”

## 7:40 — Slide 10 · Limits and next

> “What this is not: a booking product, a bank, a live-price engine or a promise of savings.
> Planning targets are illustrative; there is no backend, no accounts, no push.
> Next: savings tracking, custom swaps and dream trips, accounts and sync, end-to-end tests.
> The product is at tripswap-home.vercel.app; the code is at github.com/blamelas/tripswap.
> Thank you — I’m happy to take questions.”

## If you are running late

- Compress slides 5–6 to one sentence each.
- Keep the demo and the two AI failures; they are the evidence.
- Never skip slide 10: the limits are part of the argument.
